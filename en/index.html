Self-hosted dashboard

Everything that matters, on one screen.

PiBoard is a tile dashboard for Windows, Linux, macOS and Raspberry Pi: weather, traffic, news from RSS feeds, mail, calendar, TV guide, sports, stocks. 36 tiles to arrange on a wall-mounted touchscreen or on your computer's second monitor.

The tiles

What you check ten times a day, already on display.

36 tiles in ten families. Each one moves and resizes with a finger or a mouse; the data comes from open sources or from your own services.

Weather

Open-Meteo forecasts with a choice of models, including Météo-France and ECMWF, plus sunrise and sunset times.

Traffic and commute

Live TomTom traffic map, and commute time compared with the usual one, with a suggested departure time.

News from RSS feeds

Your RSS and Atom feeds scroll by themselves. A tap opens the article in reader mode, text extracted from the page, without leaving the board.

Mail and calendar

The latest messages from your IMAP mailbox, readable on tap and read-only, next to your merged calendars.

Crypto and stocks

Live prices with a fallback source for each market: the tile stays filled when a provider goes down.

Smart home

Connects to Home Assistant over WebSocket, falling back to the REST API automatically.

Network and system

Network status, public IP address, GPU load, with charts that share one time axis.

TV, sports and photos

French DTT and Canal+ listings, scores and standings, and your photos as a slideshow from a folder, a NAS or a USB key.

Classic mode

One board, and three drawers for everything else.

In classic mode, PiBoard shows a single board. Three collapsible drawers, on the left, top and right edges, add as many surfaces: pull a tab and the drawer slides over the board.

  • 01Tiles in a closed drawer keep working: the countdown keeps counting, scores keep refreshing.
  • 02Each drawer resizes with a finger and can have its own colour theme.
  • 03Ideal when one large tile fills the screen, a traffic map for instance: the rest waits in a drawer.
  • 04At the bottom, a tab reveals the toolbar: add a tile, edit mode, settings, built-in help.
Dashboard mode

Several pages, one screen.

When one grid is no longer enough, spread your tiles across several pages and let PiBoard cycle through them.

  • 01Sliding pages, each with its own animation.
  • 02Automatic cycling, with a duration set page by page.
  • 03A background image per page, five framings and a veil that keeps tiles readable.
Platforms

Windows, Linux, macOS, Raspberry Pi: the same app.

PiBoard was born on a Raspberry Pi, but today it is a full desktop application. Every tile works the same everywhere; all that is left is to pick the package that matches your system and your processor.

Windows

A regular installer for 64-bit PCs. The app updates itself.

x64

Linux

A .deb package for Debian, Ubuntu or Mint, or an AppImage for any distribution. Automatic updates.

x64 · arm64

macOS

A .dmg disk image, for macOS 12 and later. You update by downloading the new .dmg.

arm64 (Apple Silicon) · x64 (Intel)

Raspberry Pi

Full-screen kiosk on Raspberry Pi OS Bookworm or Trixie, for a wall-mounted touchscreen. Pi 3, 4 or 5.

arm64 · 32-bit kiosk possible

x64 or arm64: which one?

x64 (also written amd64 or x86_64): the Intel and AMD processors in PCs, and Intel Macs.

arm64: Apple Silicon Macs (M1 and later) and Raspberry Pi 3, 4 and 5 on a 64-bit system.

A package only runs on the architecture it was built for. The desktop app has no 32-bit build; a Pi on a 32-bit system uses the kiosk install.

Updates without a screwdriver.

On Windows, on Linux and on the Pi, PiBoard updates itself from GitHub Releases and backs up your data before each update. On macOS, because the app is not notarized by Apple, updating stays manual.

Open source

Free, and staying that way.

PiBoard is released under the MIT license. No paid edition, no license key, no account to create: the app runs on your hardware and your settings stay there.
