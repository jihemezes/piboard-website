Self-hosted dashboard

Everything that matters, on one screen. You needed it, you just didn't know it yet.

PiBoard is a tile dashboard for Windows, Linux, macOS and Raspberry Pi: weather, traffic, news from RSS feeds, mail, calendar, TV guide, Formula 1 and MotoGP, stocks, network monitoring. 39 tiles to arrange on a wall-mounted touchscreen or on your computer's second monitor.

The tiles

What you check ten times a day, already on display.

39 tiles in ten families. Each one moves and resizes with a finger or a mouse; the data comes from open sources or from your own services.

Weather

Open-Meteo forecasts with a choice of models, including Météo-France and ECMWF, plus sunrise and sunset times.

Traffic and commute

Live TomTom traffic map, and commute time compared with the usual one, with a suggested departure time.

News from RSS feeds

Up to 12 RSS and Atom feeds per tile, each with its own colour. A tap opens the article in reader mode, and your newspaper subscriptions open in full.

Mail and calendar

The latest messages from your IMAP mailbox, readable on tap and read-only, next to your merged calendars.

Crypto and stocks

Live prices with a fallback source for each market: the tile stays filled when a provider goes down.

Smart home

Connects to Home Assistant over WebSocket, falling back to the REST API automatically.

Network and system

An alert when an unknown device joins your network, a toolbox per device (ping, ports, traceroute, Wake-on-LAN), connection health and the state of 67 online services.

Formula 1, MotoGP and sports

The whole Grand Prix weekend — sessions, standings, weather, circuit map — in a tile you lay out yourself, plus football and rugby scores and standings.

Classic mode

One board, and three drawers for everything else.

In classic mode, PiBoard shows a single board. Three collapsible drawers, on the left, top and right edges, add as many surfaces: pull a tab and the drawer slides over the board.

  • 01Tiles in a closed drawer keep working: the countdown keeps counting, scores keep refreshing.
  • 02Each drawer resizes with a finger and can have its own colour theme.
  • 03Ideal when one large tile fills the screen, a traffic map for instance: the rest waits in a drawer.
  • 04At the bottom, a tab reveals the toolbar: add a tile, edit mode, settings, built-in help.
Dashboard mode

Several pages, one screen.

When one grid is no longer enough, spread your tiles across several pages and let PiBoard cycle through them.

  • 01Sliding pages, each with its own animation.
  • 02Automatic cycling, with a duration set page by page.
  • 03A background image per page, five framings and a veil that keeps tiles readable.

Day to day

Everything is set on screen, no file to edit.

With a mouse or a finger, PiBoard is arranged, decorated and explained from inside the app itself.

Right-click a tile

A menu for that tile alone: settings, refresh, move or resize it without touching the others, full screen, lock, send it to another page.

Pages that organise themselves

Reorder and duplicate pages, send a tile from one page to another, export a whole page to take it to another machine.

Gestures and touch

Change page with a swipe on the trackpad, the Magic Mouse or the side buttons. On a touchscreen, an on-screen keyboard and large-key number pads.

Themes and relief

Over fifty themes and an editor to make your own, automatic day and night, and tiles in relief with shadows, light and optional frosted glass.

Your images and photos

An image library you fill by drag and drop, folders picked in a window with nothing to type, and the Mac Photos library, albums included.

Built-in settings and help

Settings sorted into seven families, with search, and a full-page help that details every tile and each of its options, in French and English.

Platforms

Windows, Linux, macOS, Raspberry Pi: the same app.

PiBoard was born on a Raspberry Pi, but today it is a full desktop application. Every tile works the same everywhere; all that is left is to pick the package that matches your system and your processor.

Windows

A regular installer for 64-bit PCs. The app updates itself.

x64

Linux

A .deb package for Debian, Ubuntu or Mint, or an AppImage for any distribution. Automatic updates.

x64 · arm64

macOS

A .dmg disk image signed and notarized by Apple: it opens with no warning. macOS 12 and later, automatic updates.

arm64 (Apple Silicon) · x64 (Intel)

Raspberry Pi

Full-screen kiosk on Raspberry Pi OS Bookworm or Trixie, for a wall-mounted touchscreen. Pi 3, 4 or 5.

arm64 · 32-bit kiosk possible

x64 or arm64: which one?

x64 (also written amd64 or x86_64): the Intel and AMD processors in PCs, and Intel Macs.

arm64: Apple Silicon Macs (M1 and later) and Raspberry Pi 3, 4 and 5 on a 64-bit system.

A package only runs on the architecture it was built for. The desktop app has no 32-bit build; a Pi on a 32-bit system uses the kiosk install.

Updates without a screwdriver.

On Windows, Linux, macOS and the Pi, PiBoard updates itself from GitHub Releases and backs up your data before each update. The packages for all three desktop systems are built automatically for every new version.

Open source

Free, and staying that way.

PiBoard is released under the MIT license. No paid edition, no license key, no account to create: the app runs on your hardware and your settings stay there.
